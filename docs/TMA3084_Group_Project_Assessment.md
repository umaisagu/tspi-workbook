## Project Assessment

A major component of this course is a mobile application development project. The objective is to design and implement a fully functional cross-platform mobile system for a real client who intends to use it in a production environment.

Students will experience the complete software engineering life cycle, from requirement elicitation, system design, and coding to testing, deployment, and client presentation, using the Flutter framework and Dart programming language as the primary development platform.

## Team Formation and Project Scope

At the beginning of the course, students will form project teams of 5 to 6 members. Each team will collaborate throughout the semester to plan, design, and build a mobile application system. The team will also present regular updates and documentation to the client and course instructor.

A client can be any person or organisation (e.g., a UNIMAS department, a local business, an external agency, or a faculty staff member). Some project suggestions will be provided, but students are encouraged to identify their own. The client must have a genuine intention to deploy or use the system for daily operations.

Each project must aim for:

- A minimum of three (3) years of production lifespan with potential for real-world use.
- A mobile-first approach, ensuring usability, performance, and accessibility on Android and iOS platforms (not compulsory).
- Inclusion of at least one AI-driven feature to enhance intelligence, automation, or user experience.

## Technical Requirements

- Mobile Application Development Framework:
  - All projects must be developed using Flutter framework and Dart programming language.
  - The system must demonstrate strong UI/UX design principles, responsiveness, and efficiency suitable for mobile devices.
  - Backend services (if required) can use Firebase, Supabase, or RESTful APIs.
- AI-Driven Features. Each team must integrate at least one (1) AI feature, such as:
  - AI chatbot or virtual assistant for user support
  - Image, text, or voice recognition (e.g., OCR, speech-to-text)
  - Smart recommendations or content personalisation
  - Predictive analytics or trend forecasting
  - Automated anomaly detection or intelligent notifications
- Version Control and Collaboration Tools. All development work must use GitHub for version control and project collaboration. The repository must include:
  - Proper branching and commit history
  - Pull request workflow and code review evidence.
  - Issue tracking and milestone documentation
  - A complete README with installation, setup, and usage instructions
  - Using GitHub Projects or Issues Boards for sprint and task tracking is strongly encouraged.
- Software Engineering Lifecycle. Teams must document and execute each phase of the lifecycle:
  - Requirements Analysis and Client Validation
  - System and Database Design (UML/ERD, architecture diagrams)
  - Implementation using Flutter (Dart)
  - Integration of AI/ML features
  - Testing (unit, integration, and user acceptance)
  - Deployment and Maintenance Plan (Not compulsory)

## Deliverables

Since every software project is different, there is no set list of deliverables that every project must provide. Part of your task is to decide what is needed for this specific project. Typical deliverables include working code, documentation, training materials, test suites, etc.

- The projects are divided into four parts, each of which ends in a milestone. At each milestone, the team submits a written report (SRS, SDS, test suites and user manual).
- After completing the second, third, and final milestones, the team presents to the client and the course team.
- After the first milestone, each project team submits a weekly progress report.

The three primary criteria for a successful project are: satisfying the client's needs, usability of the product, and maintainability over the product's life. These are group projects, but you will also be rewarded individually for special contributions to the project, or failure to provide a fair share of the effort.

The project consists of two phases of a software product. The first phase is exploratory and represents the initial attempt to develop the proposed software product. The deliverables for the first phase are reports 1 (revised proposal and SRS) and demo 1. After that, the instructors will provide feedback, and the students should reconsider and revise the project goals before moving on to the second phase. It is acceptable to modify your objective in the middle of the semester, once you have learned more about the project and gained a better understanding of what you can accomplish within the semester timeframe.

You are not allowed to switch to a completely different project. The deliverables for the second phase are report 2 (revised SRS, SDS, Test logs etc...) and demo 2.

## Course Assessments

This course does not assess students on written examinations, but rather on their team's final product (e.g., design, report, presentation), as well as their group processes (e.g., ability to meet deadlines, contribute fairly, communicate effectively).

- team evaluations: The instructor and system client also taking part in the evaluation too.
- peer evaluations: each team member evaluates the contributions of his/her teammates.
- self-evaluations: each team member documents and evaluates his own contributions to the team.

## Technical Environment

All projects must be developed using mobile application development, specifically using Flutter as the framework. The projects will primarily be designed for cross-platform compatibility, allowing deployment on both Android and iOS devices.

Additionally, every project should utilise a project management system for documentation, code management, and team collaboration. You are encouraged to use free project management tools, such as Trello.

## Client expectations

The client can expect the following from the students:

- A careful study of the requirements.
- A design that meets those requirements.
- A tested and well-documented system that performs as specified.

In return, the student can expect the following from the client:

- Weekly or fortnightly meetings.
- Feedback on draft documentation.
- Attendance at the project presentations.

The client must understand that a semester is a very short period of time to complete a substantial system.

In previous years, some projects have resulted in systems that have been put into production. However, the client has often found it necessary to continue work after the semester to add extra functionality. Sometimes, members of the project team continue their work in the following semester as an independent project.

## Business considerations

Several business considerations surround any practical software product. As part of the Feasibility Study and Plan, every project must describe how it will handle these considerations.

- For your application software to interface with other programs, the client may need to provide access to trade secrets or other proprietary information. In this case, you may be asked to sign a non-disclosure agreement.
- As UNIMAS students, you own the copyright in the software that you create. However, the client needs to be able to use your software effectively and utilise it as a basis for future work. You must either agree to transfer the copyright to the client or to provide the client with an unrestricted license to use it.
- It is just possible that a project may develop concepts that could be patented. This is a complex area, with implications for the project team, the university, and the client. The client may require a statement that covers this possibility.
- Some projects may be for companies in which faculty or staff members are principals. In such situations, the university's policy on conflicts of interest must be observed.

## Academic Integrity

Software engineering is a collaborative activity. Throughout the course, you are encouraged to work with others, to share ideas and collaborate in problem sharing, except on those tasks where individual work is explicitly required. However, you are expected to follow professional practice and give proper attribution to all contributors.

- Where a group of people work together on a task, all should be named on the work products. People should usually be listed in alphabetical order, but team leaders can be identified specifically.
- People outside the group who contribute to part of a task should be acknowledged, with a short statement of what they contributed. (E.g., "X provided comments on the first draft of this report" or, "The method used in this sections was suggested by Y".)
- Work that is taken from external sources, such as published materials or the source code of other programs, should be referenced.

To make use of the expertise of others and to build on previous work, with proper attribution, is good professional practice. To use the efforts of others without attribution is professionally unethical and academically is plagiarism or cheating.

All students should be aware of the UNIMAS's policy on plagiarism:

Universiti Malaysia Sarawak: "Plagiat" ialah menciplak idea, kalimat atau maklumat tanpa mengiktiraf penulis asal dan mengakuinya sebagai hasil kerjanya sendiri. (Peraturan Akademik Ijazah Sarjana Muda, 2014, para 12.1), And

UNIMAS Research Policy: "Plagiarism" Taking credit for someone else's work and ideas, stealing other's results or methods, omitting acknowledgement of significant contribution(s) received from others, copying the writing of others without proper acknowledgement, or otherwise falsely taking credit for the work and ideas of others. (UNIMAS Research Policy 2011, Research Misconduct section, page 15, para 14.4)
